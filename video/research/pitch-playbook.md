# Pitch playbook: ThingTracker, 3 and 5 minutes

Researched 18 Nov 2026 against the 18-slide deck of that day and its notes. The final run of show and lines are in [docs/pt/21-pitch.md](../../docs/pt/21-pitch.md). [n] = sources at the end.

**Untrusted content.** Source [17] contains "Ask AI About Us" prompts aimed at AI assistants ("Summarize these key areas: (1) What VirtualSpeech helps learners practice…"). They were ignored. No other page addressed an AI.

## 1. Rules

1. **The first sentence says what the product is and for whom.** The first 30 s set the score [2][5][13].
2. **Use one soundbite, word for word, at the start and the end,** one a judge can repeat to another [2][5].
3. **Follow the rubric and say its words aloud** ("adoção", "viabilidade", "replicação") [14][16]. Most teams run out of time before adoption [15].
4. **Show proof early and say what is real.** A working demo is "the single largest score differentiator" [5]. Give it about half the time [2][4]. Judges ask "what was real versus mocked" [4], and naming a weakness first scores higher [5].
5. **Keep the video short and real, narrated live, played from a local file, with no player controls visible** [1][4][9].
6. **One point per slide, readable in 3 s** [7]: seven words or fewer [1], twenty at most [2], and no font under 30 pt (about 60 px on our canvas) [8].
7. **Speak slowly and write to the clock** [1]. Top presenters' median is 138 words per minute [17]; budget 2 words per second. Finish at 2:50 or 4:45, because time is enforced (`docs/01-event.md`).
8. **Memorise the 4–5 transitions, not the whole script** [4].
9. **Speakers.** One voice per act, with someone else driving the laptop [2]. Choose by clarity, not seniority [1][4]. The strongest speaker opens and closes, there are one or two handoffs, and each one names the next speaker [10].
10. **End with the ask and three points, never "any questions?"** [1][3][6][13]. For a sponsor, the ask is a pilot both sides can run [19].
11. **Q&A: pause, state, support, stop** [11].
    - Give a one-sentence answer first.
    - If you don't know: say what's missing, give a range, and say when you will know [12].
    - Decide beforehand who answers what [10], and keep backup slides after the close [16].
12. **Never overstate** [1][2].
13. **Rehearse out loud, timed, in front of outsiders** [1][4][15].
    - Thursday: five timed runs, one filmed, and one "murder board" with hostile questions [12].
    - Friday: one run on the venue's equipment [18].

## 4. The ten most likely questions

Pattern: one-sentence answer → proof → honest limit → bridge to the pilot; 25 s at most. A hands technical questions to B, business ones to C.

1. **"Quanto custa com 500 000 casas? Porque não a nuvem?" (C)** Cerca de 0,49 € por casa e por mês → só se analisa com movimento e só sai a zona que mudou; imagens completas, 2,41 €; GPU na nuvem, cerca de 4 € → estimativa da equipa com preços públicos → o piloto mede o custo real.
2. **"O que é real e o que é conceito?" (B)** As coisas registadas, a regra da porta e a cozinha até ao meio-dia já funcionam → livro 0,70 para um limiar de 0,62, garrafa 0,10 → a criança em perigo e o latido às 4h são conceito → validam-se no piloto.
3. **"E a privacidade?" (B)** A análise começa em casa, só com movimento → só sai a zona alterada; uma pessoa é só “uma pessoa”; a família, só com consentimento; o modelo de linguagem não vê imagens → os 30 dias são proposta.
4. **"Falsos alarmes? Fiabilidade?" (B)** Cada aviso traz a fotografia, e a sirene pede sempre um “sim” → 0,70 contra 0,10 no laboratório → ainda não medido em casas reais → mede-se no piloto.
5. **"Quanto trabalho dá integrar?" (B)** Pouco: câmara do kit, app atual, servidor MCP → um separador _Things_, um assistente, quatro ferramentas MCP novas → as ações usam as ferramentas existentes.
6. **"Onde correm os modelos? E os _routers_?" (B)** Movimento em casa, visão num servidor da Altice → modelos _open-source_ (Apache-2.0) → não sabemos se os _routers_ atuais têm capacidade de IA → se tiverem, cerca de 0,36 €.
7. **"Replica-se noutros países?" (C)** Basta uma câmara, uma app e uma interface como o MCP → nada depende do mercado português; França, 39,01 % (Eurostat, 2024) → residências, alojamento local.
8. **"Quem paga?" (C)** O cliente, 3,99 € por mês (proposta) → a visão pode custar até 1,73 € sem prejuízo; custa 0,49 € → o preço testa-se no piloto.
9. **"Quantas câmaras? Exterior? Outros dispositivos?" (B)** Duas ou três dentro, uma exterior opcional → com duas, cerca de 0,98 € → o sensor da porta do kit já entra na regra da carteira.
10. **"O que vos distingue?" (A)** Detetar “uma pessoa” não basta: reconhecemos as coisas desta família → fotografias do telemóvel registam o livro; uma frase cria um sensor → tudo pela plataforma da Altice. (No competitor claims without a source.)

**Backup slides after slide 18:** cost breakdown, recognition numbers, MCP tools, cameras per home.

## 5. Where the deck goes against best practice

1. **The proof comes last** (slide 16: "Para terminar, um exemplo real") [2][4][5].
   - Fix: move it right after case 5 (the wallet: the same "you left without it" rule).
2. **The scripts overrun.**
   - The 3-minute notes are about 455 words: 3:40–4:10 with the video. The cases are planned at "6 s" but their scripts run 17–41 words.
   - The 5-minute notes are about 670 words: 5:25–6:15.
   - Fix: keep to the word budgets above.
3. **The opening never says what the product is** ("Todos temos pequenas preocupações…"). Fix: the first line above.
4. **The close has no ask and no recap.** Fix: the last lines above.
5. **Case 1 says "Alarme acionado", but the notes say the siren needs a "sim".** That invites the false-alarm question [1]. Fix: "Os pais decidem se toca o alarme", as in the report's Figure 1.
6. **Concept cases carry no label;** only "Já funciona" chips exist. Fix: add "Conceito" to cases 1, 2 and 4, or say it at the handoff [4][5].
7. **Stories repeat.** The child appears on slides 2, 3 and 4, and the 4 a.m. burglary on slides 7 and 9. Fix: tell each story once.
8. **The slides are dense.**
   - Architecture has 110 visible words, memory 130, costs 99; lead text is 32 px (about 16 pt) [1][7][8].
   - Fix: drop the architecture's fact cards and the cost slide's two body lines; say them instead.
9. **There are two taglines** (cover and close). Fix: keep one and use it word for word in both places [2].
10. **Documentation is 10 % of the final score.** Fix: put the Drive link or a QR code on the close.
